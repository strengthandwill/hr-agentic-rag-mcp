Data Security & Acceptable Use Policy

Document ID: POL-SEC-05
Category: Security & Compliance
Effective Date: 2026-01-01
Owner: IT Security Team
Applies to: All CPDA employees and contractors

Fictional company policy created for this course project. Synthetic data only.

1. Scope

This policy governs the acceptable use of CPDA-issued devices, accounts, and access to CPDA systems and data, including citizen and internal data handled in the course of work.

2. Device Requirements

All devices used to access CPDA systems must have full-disk encryption enabled, a CPDA-managed endpoint security agent installed, automatic screen lock after 5 minutes of inactivity, and must be kept on a supported, patched operating system version. Personal devices may only access email and calendar via the approved mobile management profile; they may not be used to access production systems containing personal data.

3. Remote and Overseas Access

When connecting from outside a CPDA office, employees must use the CPDA VPN at all times while accessing internal systems. Access to production systems containing personal data (as defined in the Data Classification Standard) from overseas locations is subject to additional review by IT Security, and may be temporarily restricted for destinations on the IT Security watch list due to data-residency or legal-access risk. Employees working overseas per the Remote Work & FWA Policy (POL-FWA-03) must complete the Overseas Work Notification so IT Security can assess and, where needed, adjust access controls in advance.

4. Data Classification

  • Public — approved for external release (e.g. published policies).
  • Internal — for CPDA staff use only (e.g. internal memos, org charts).
  • Confidential — sensitive business data (e.g. vendor contracts).
  • Restricted — personal data of employees or members of the public; requires need-to-know access, encryption at rest and in transit, and audit logging.

5. Acceptable Use

CPDA accounts and systems are provided for business use. Incidental personal use (e.g. checking personal email during breaks) is permitted provided it does not involve illegal activity, does not consume significant system resources, and does not involve installing unapproved software. Sharing of login credentials, disabling security agents, or exfiltrating Confidential or Restricted data to personal accounts or unapproved cloud storage is strictly prohibited and may result in disciplinary action under the Workplace Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09).

6. Incident Reporting

Suspected security incidents (lost device, phishing, suspected unauthorised access) must be reported to IT Security within 24 hours via the IT Service Desk. Delayed reporting may increase the severity of any resulting disciplinary or remediation action.

7. Password and Authentication Requirements

All CPDA accounts require multi-factor authentication (MFA) where the underlying system supports it. Passwords must be at least 12 characters and are not required to be rotated on a fixed schedule, consistent with current best practice, provided MFA is enabled and no compromise is suspected. Password managers are encouraged; sharing passwords between colleagues, even temporarily to cover an absence, is prohibited under Section 5.

8. Third-Party and Cloud Services

Employees may not upload Confidential or Restricted data (as defined in Section 4) to personal cloud storage, personal email, or any third-party AI or productivity tool that has not been reviewed and approved by IT Security. A list of approved tools is maintained by IT Security and shared during onboarding (POL-ONB-07 §3) and updated periodically; when in doubt, ask IT Security before using a new external tool with CPDA data.

9. Physical Security

Laptops must not be left unattended and unlocked in public spaces, including cafes, airports, and co-working spaces. When working from a CPDA office, employees should use a privacy screen in open seating areas when working with Confidential or Restricted data where visible to passers-by.

10. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Lost phone. An employee's CPDA-enrolled mobile phone is lost at a restaurant. They must report this to the IT Service Desk within 24 hours per Section 6 so the device can be remotely wiped via the mobile management profile; delaying the report increases risk exposure and may affect any follow-up disciplinary review.

Example 2 — Suspicious email. An employee receives an email that looks like it is from their manager asking them to purchase gift cards urgently. This is a classic phishing pattern; per Section 6 it should be reported to IT Security immediately rather than acted upon, even if the sender name looks correct.

Example 3 — New AI coding tool. An engineer wants to use a new AI-assisted coding tool that is not on the approved list. Per Section 8, they should request IT Security review before connecting it to any CPDA repository or pasting Confidential code into it, since many such tools retain submitted data on external servers.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I use my personal laptop to read CPDA email if I don't have my work laptop with me?
A: Only through the approved mobile/email management profile described in Section 2; personal devices may not access production systems containing personal data regardless of the reason.

Q: Is it OK to connect to public Wi-Fi at a cafe while working remotely?
A: Yes, provided the CPDA VPN is active at all times while accessing internal systems, per Section 3 — the VPN requirement exists specifically to make public or untrusted networks safe to use.

Q: Do the overseas access restrictions in Section 3 apply to every country?
A: Only destinations on the IT Security watch list face additional restrictions; most destinations only require the standard Overseas Work Notification under the Remote Work & FWA Policy (POL-FWA-03 §4) with no extra access limitation.

Q: What counts as "Restricted" data in practice?
A: Any personal data of employees or members of the public — for example, employee PTO balances, benefits elections, or HR case details — is Restricted under Section 4 and requires need-to-know access, encryption, and audit logging.
