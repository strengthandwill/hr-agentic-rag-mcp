# Benefits & Medical Insurance Policy

**Document ID:** POL-BEN-06
**Category:** Benefits
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies to:** CPDA employees (Singapore); eligibility varies by employment type

> Fictional company policy created for this course project. Synthetic data only.

## 1. Eligibility by Employment Type

| Employment Type | Medical & Dental | Life/Personal Accident Insurance | Wellness Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-time, confirmed | Full coverage | Covered | SGD 400/year |
| Full-time, probation | Emergency-only coverage | Covered | Not yet eligible |
| Part-time (>= 0.5 FTE) | Pro-rated coverage | Covered | SGD 200/year |
| Contractor | Not eligible (see contract) | Not eligible | Not eligible |
| Intern | Emergency-only coverage | Covered | Not eligible |

Confirmed full-time employees become eligible for full medical and dental coverage on their
confirmation date (typically after a 3-month probation period).

## 2. Medical Coverage

CPDA provides outpatient medical coverage (GP visits, specialist referrals) and hospitalisation
& surgical insurance for confirmed employees, administered through the CPDA-appointed insurer.
Dependents (spouse, children) may be added to dental and outpatient coverage at the employee's
own cost via payroll deduction; hospitalisation coverage for dependents is subsidised at 50%.

## 3. Dental Coverage

Confirmed employees receive an annual dental benefit of up to **SGD 300** for routine care
(cleaning, fillings) at panel clinics, or partial reimbursement for non-panel clinics.

## 4. Wellness Benefit

Confirmed full-time employees may claim up to **SGD 400 per calendar year** toward gym
memberships, fitness classes, or approved wellness apps. Part-time employees receive a
pro-rated SGD 200/year. This benefit does not carry forward between years.

## 5. CPF Contributions

For Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents, CPDA contributes to the Central Provident Fund
(CPF) at the prevailing statutory employer rate, in addition to the employee's own contribution
deducted from payroll, consistent with Singapore CPF Board requirements.

## 6. Benefits Status Changes

Life events (marriage, birth of a child, change in employment type) should be reported to
People & Culture within 30 days so that dependent coverage and benefits status can be updated.
Benefits questions specific to an individual's coverage should be verified against that
employee's current benefits election record, since coverage tiers can change after confirmation,
promotion, or a switch between full-time and part-time status.

## 7. Opt-Out and Waiver

Employees who have equivalent coverage through a spouse's employer or other means may waive
CPDA outpatient medical coverage, but not hospitalisation & surgical insurance, which is
mandatory for all confirmed employees. Waivers do not result in a cash payout and must be
re-elected annually during the benefits open enrolment window.

## 8. Claims Process

Outpatient claims at panel clinics are typically settled automatically with no paperwork from
the employee (the clinic bills the insurer directly). For non-panel clinics or claims requiring
reimbursement, employees submit a claim with the original receipt and diagnosis code through the
HR benefits portal within 90 days of the visit.

## 9. Worked Examples

**Example 1 — New dependent after marriage.** An employee marries and wants to add their spouse
to dental and outpatient coverage. They report the life event to People & Culture within 30 days
(Section 6), after which the spouse can be added via payroll deduction; hospitalisation coverage
for the spouse is available at the standard 50% subsidy.

**Example 2 — Part-time wellness benefit.** A part-time employee at 0.6 FTE wants to confirm
their wellness benefit cap. Per Section 4, part-time employees receive a pro-rated SGD 200/year
(versus SGD 400/year for full-time), regardless of exact FTE fraction — the part-time tier is a
flat SGD 200, not further pro-rated by FTE percentage.

**Example 3 — Probation period medical visit.** An employee on probation needs to see a
specialist for a non-emergency condition. Per Section 1, probationary staff have emergency-only
coverage, so this visit would not be covered under the CPDA plan; the employee may still see the
specialist privately at their own cost, with full coverage beginning on confirmation.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When exactly does my full medical coverage start?**
A: On your confirmation date, which is typically the end of the standard 3-month probation
period (see Onboarding Checklist POL-ONB-07 §5), not on your original start date.

**Q: Can I claim the wellness benefit for a one-time purchase, like running shoes?**
A: The wellness benefit is intended for gym memberships, fitness classes, and approved wellness
apps; one-time equipment purchases are generally not covered, though a benefits portal FAQ or
People & Culture can confirm edge cases.

**Q: Does the dental benefit cover orthodontics (e.g. braces)?**
A: The standard SGD 300/year dental benefit in Section 3 covers routine care; orthodontic
treatment typically exceeds this annual cap and is only partially offset, with the remainder
being the employee's responsibility.

**Q: I changed from part-time to full-time mid-year. Does my benefits tier update
immediately?**
A: Yes, employment-type changes are reflected in your benefits tier from the effective date of
the change, reported to People & Culture per Section 6, though the wellness benefit cap for that
calendar year is not retroactively adjusted for months already elapsed at the prior tier.
