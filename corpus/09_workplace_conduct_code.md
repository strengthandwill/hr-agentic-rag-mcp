# Workplace Conduct Code

**Document ID:** POL-CONDUCT-09
**Category:** Conduct & Compliance
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies to:** All CPDA employees, contractors, and interns

> Fictional company policy created for this course project. Synthetic data only.

## 1. Core Values

CPDA employees are expected to act in ways consistent with the CPDA Way: **Agile** (respond to
change with curiosity, not defensiveness), **Open** (share information and give direct,
respectful feedback), and **Trusted** (do what you say, protect the people and data in your
care).

## 2. Respectful Workplace

Harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation of any kind — based on race, gender, age,
religion, disability, sexual orientation, or any other protected characteristic — is prohibited.
This applies to interactions in the office, remote/virtual meetings, and work-related social
events.

## 3. Reporting a Concern

Employees who experience or witness a conduct concern should report it to their manager, People
& Culture, or via the confidential HR case channel. Reports are treated as confidential to the
extent possible while allowing CPDA to investigate. Retaliation against anyone who raises a
concern in good faith is itself a violation of this policy.

## 4. Escalation and Investigation

- **Low-severity, ambiguous, or first-reported interpersonal friction** is typically handled
  directly by the manager or People & Culture with coaching or mediation.
- **Sensitive matters** — harassment, discrimination, safety concerns, suspected policy
  violations involving data or finances — must be escalated to People & Culture for formal
  intake and, where appropriate, a documented HR case, rather than resolved informally. See the
  **HR Case Intake & Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11)** for the triage criteria People &
  Culture uses.
- People & Culture will acknowledge a formal report within 3 working days and provide an
  expected timeline for next steps.

## 5. Disciplinary Process

Confirmed violations may result in outcomes ranging from a verbal coaching conversation to
formal warnings, performance improvement plans, or termination, depending on severity and
history, following a fair process that includes the employee's right to respond.

## 6. Conflicts of Interest

Employees must disclose outside employment, financial interests, or personal relationships that
could reasonably create a conflict of interest with their CPDA role, particularly in
procurement, hiring, or vendor-facing work.
