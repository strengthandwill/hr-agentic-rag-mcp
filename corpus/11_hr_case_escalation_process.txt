HR CASE INTAKE & ESCALATION PROCESS

Document ID: POL-HRTRIAGE-11
Category: Conduct & Compliance
Effective Date: 2026-01-01
Owner: People & Culture Team
Applies to: All CPDA employees

NOTE: Fictional company policy created for this course project. Synthetic data only.

1. PURPOSE

This document describes how People & Culture triages incoming HR cases -- questions,
complaints, or concerns raised by employees -- to decide whether a case can be answered
directly with policy information, needs a mock HR ticket opened for tracking, or must be
escalated to a human HR case officer.

2. SEVERITY TIERS

Tier 1 - Informational: A general policy question with a clear answer in the policy corpus
(e.g. "how many annual leave days do I get"). Can be answered directly, with citations, by
the HR assistant or People & Culture, without opening a case.

Tier 2 - Actionable Request: A request that requires looking up the employee's own data and
may require a follow-up action, such as a PTO request, an expense question tied to the
employee's role, or a benefits question tied to enrollment status. These may result in a mock
HR ticket being opened so a human can follow up, but do not require immediate escalation.

Tier 3 - Sensitive / Escalate: Any report touching harassment, discrimination, safety, or a
significant suspected policy violation (see Workplace Conduct Code POL-CONDUCT-09). These
must always be escalated to a human People & Culture case officer. An automated assistant
should not attempt to adjudicate the situation, should express empathy, point to the
Workplace Conduct Code, and create a mock HR case summary for a human to follow up on -- never
closing the loop with a definitive resolution on its own.

3. ESCALATION CRITERIA CHECKLIST

A request should be escalated to Tier 3 if it involves any of the following:
- Allegations of harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation.
- Safety concerns (physical or psychological).
- Suspected fraud, data breach, or serious policy violation.
- Requests explicitly asking to speak to a human HR officer.
- Situations where the employee expresses distress and the assistant cannot fully resolve the
  concern with policy information alone.

4. HANDLING AMBIGUOUS REQUESTS

If a request is ambiguous (e.g. missing employee ID, unclear which policy applies, or unclear
whether it is Tier 1, 2, or 3), the assistant or HR staff member should ask a clarifying
question before taking any action, rather than guessing. This is especially important before
creating a ticket or drafting any communication on the employee's behalf.

5. MOCK HR TICKET CREATION

For Tier 2 and Tier 3 cases, a mock HR ticket may be created to track follow-up. Mock ticket
creation is a reversible, non-binding administrative action -- it does not itself resolve the
issue -- but it should still only be created after the employee has confirmed they want a
ticket opened, since it may result in a human HR staff member contacting them.

6. CASE DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS

Every Tier 2 or Tier 3 case, whether or not a formal ticket is created, should be documented
with: the date and channel of the report, a factual (non-judgmental) summary of what was
reported, the tier assigned and why, and the next action taken. This documentation standard
exists so that a human People & Culture officer reviewing the case later has enough context to
act without having to re-interview the employee from scratch.

7. ROLE OF THE AUTOMATED HR ASSISTANT

An automated assistant (such as this system) may answer Tier 1 questions directly with policy
citations, gather information and draft a Tier 2 or Tier 3 case summary for human review, and
explain escalation options to the employee. It must not: promise a specific disciplinary outcome,
tell the employee a Tier 3 matter has been "resolved," or take any action (such as creating a
case or drafting a message) without the employee's explicit confirmation, consistent with the
Agentic System Design safety guardrails this system implements.

8. WORKED EXAMPLES

Example 1 -- Tier 1 handled directly. An employee asks "how many Annual Leave days do I get
after 5 years." This is answered directly with a citation to the Annual Leave Policy
(POL-PTO-01), no case or ticket needed.

Example 2 -- Tier 2 with optional ticket. An employee asks whether a laptop expense is
reimbursable and wants someone to double check their specific situation. The assistant answers
the policy question directly and offers to open a mock HR ticket (category: expense) for a human
to review the specific claim, only after the employee confirms they want that.

Example 3 -- Tier 3 escalation. An employee describes a colleague repeatedly making them
uncomfortable. Per the escalation criteria in Section 3, this is immediately treated as Tier 3:
the assistant responds with empathy, cites the Workplace Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09), makes
clear a human People & Culture officer must be involved, and offers (with confirmation) to
create a mock HR case summary rather than attempting to resolve the situation itself.

9. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: Can a Tier 1 question become a Tier 3 case partway through the conversation?
A: Yes. If an employee asking a general policy question subsequently discloses something
matching the Tier 3 criteria in Section 3, the conversation should be re-triaged to Tier 3 from
that point forward, regardless of how it started.

Q: Does every Tier 2 request result in a ticket being created?
A: No. Tickets are optional for Tier 2 and are only created with the employee's explicit
confirmation, per Section 5; many Tier 2 questions are fully resolved by a direct, cited answer
with no ticket needed.

Q: Who has access to a Tier 3 case summary once created?
A: Access is limited to People & Culture staff handling the case, consistent with the
need-to-know principle in the Data Security & Acceptable Use Policy (POL-SEC-05 Section 4) for
Restricted personal data.

Q: What if an employee asks the assistant to just tell them who is "right" in a workplace
dispute?
A: The assistant should decline to adjudicate and instead follow the Tier 3 escalation path in
Section 2-3, consistent with Section 7's limits on what an automated assistant may do.
