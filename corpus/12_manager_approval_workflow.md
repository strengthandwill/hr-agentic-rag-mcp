# Manager Approval & Escalation Workflow

**Document ID:** POL-APPROVAL-12
**Category:** Operations
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies to:** All CPDA employees

> Fictional company policy created for this course project. Synthetic data only.

## 1. Purpose

This document describes who approves common HR requests, and what happens when the usual
approver (the employee's direct manager) is unavailable.

## 2. Standard Approval Routing

| Request Type | Approver | Policy Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Annual Leave (PTO) | Direct manager | POL-PTO-01 |
| Family & Caregiving Leave | Direct manager, People & Culture notified | POL-FAM-10 |
| Extended/overseas remote work | Direct manager (+ Head of Dept & People & Culture if 6+ weeks) | POL-FWA-03 |
| Expense claims | Direct manager, then Finance | POL-EXP-04 |
| Home office / equipment requests | Direct manager, then IT Operations | POL-EQP-08 |

## 3. Manager Unavailable

If an employee's direct manager is unavailable (leave, vacancy) for more than 3 working days,
approvals route automatically to the manager's own manager ("skip-level") until the direct
manager returns or a new manager is assigned in the HR system.

## 4. Missing or Unclear Manager-of-Record

If the HR system does not have a manager-of-record for an employee (e.g. data entry lag for a
new hire), People & Culture acts as the interim approver for time-sensitive requests (Annual
Leave, Family Leave) until the record is corrected. Non-time-sensitive requests should wait for
the record to be fixed rather than being approved informally.

## 5. Escalation for HR Case Matters

Approval routing for standard requests (leave, expenses, equipment) is separate from the
sensitive-case escalation process. For workplace conduct or safety concerns, see the **HR Case
Intake & Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11)**, which always routes Tier 3 matters to a human
People & Culture case officer regardless of the employee's manager-of-record.

## 6. Delegation of Authority

Managers going on Annual Leave for more than 3 working days should proactively delegate approval
authority to a named colleague (not necessarily their own manager) via the HR system's
delegation feature, rather than relying on the automatic skip-level fallback in Section 3. This
keeps approvals closer to the team with direct context, while the skip-level fallback remains
available as a safety net if no delegation was set up.

## 7. Approval SLAs

| Request Type | Target Response Time |
|---|---|
| Annual Leave (POL-PTO-01) | 2 working days |
| Family & Caregiving Leave (POL-FAM-10) | 2 working days |
| Extended/overseas remote work (POL-FWA-03) | 3 working days (5 if Head of Dept sign-off needed) |
| Expense claims (POL-EXP-04) | 3 working days |
| Equipment requests (POL-EQP-08) | 3 working days |

These are target service levels, not guarantees; the skip-level and interim-approver provisions
in Sections 3-4 exist specifically to keep time-sensitive requests moving when a target is at
risk of being missed.

## 8. Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Manager on leave, no delegation set.** An employee submits a PTO request while
their manager is on a 2-week vacation with no delegate configured. Per Section 3, since the
absence exceeds 3 working days, the request automatically routes to the manager's own manager
(skip-level) until the original manager returns.

**Example 2 — New hire, manager-of-record not yet set.** A new hire's HR record has not yet been
linked to a manager due to data-entry lag. They need to request Annual Leave urgently. Per
Section 4, People & Culture acts as the interim approver for this time-sensitive request rather
than making the employee wait for the record to be corrected.

**Example 3 — Delegated approval used correctly.** A manager going on a 10-day trip delegates
approval authority to a senior teammate before leaving, per Section 6. An expense claim submitted
during the trip is approved by the delegate directly, without needing to fall back to the
skip-level manager.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can an employee request a specific person (other than their manager or the skip-level) to
approve their leave?**
A: Only if that person has been formally set up as a delegate per Section 6, or is the
designated interim approver under Section 4; approval authority is not transferable informally.

**Q: What happens to approvals already pending when a manager goes on leave?**
A: Pending approvals follow the same routing as new requests — if a delegate is set up
(Section 6), they inherit pending items; otherwise the skip-level fallback in Section 3 applies
once the 3-working-day threshold is reached.

**Q: Do approval SLAs in Section 7 apply to HR case escalations as well?**
A: No. Sensitive HR case matters follow the separate Tier-based process described in the HR Case
Intake & Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11), which has its own acknowledgment timeline rather
than the operational SLAs in this document.
