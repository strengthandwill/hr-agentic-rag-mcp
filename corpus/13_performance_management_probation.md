# Performance Management & Probation Policy

**Document ID:** POL-PERF-13
**Category:** Performance & Development
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies to:** All CPDA employees (Singapore)

> CPDA is a fictional Singapore government-technology agency created for this course project.
> All policies, figures, and data in this document are synthetic and for educational use only.

## 1. Purpose

This policy describes how performance is managed at CivicPulse Digital Agency (CPDA), including
the probation process for new hires, ongoing performance reviews, and performance improvement
plans (PIPs). It complements the 30/60/90-day goal-setting process described in the Onboarding
Checklist (POL-ONB-07 §4).

## 2. Probation Period

New full-time hires serve a standard 3-month probation period from their confirmed start date.
During probation:

- Annual Leave does not accrue (POL-PTO-01 §2), though unpaid leave may be requested.
- Benefits coverage is emergency-only (POL-BEN-06 §1).
- The employee and manager meet at least once during probation (typically around the 6-week
  mark) for an informal check-in, separate from the formal confirmation review.

Near the end of the probation period, the manager submits a confirmation review covering role
fit, performance against initial expectations, and any concerns raised during probation. Based
on this review, one of three outcomes applies: confirmation (the standard outcome), an extension
of up to 3 months in consultation with People & Culture, or, in rare cases, termination of
employment during probation with appropriate notice per the employment contract.

## 3. Ongoing Performance Reviews

Confirmed employees participate in a performance review cycle twice per year (mid-year and
year-end). Each cycle includes:

1. **Self-assessment** — the employee reflects on goals achieved, challenges faced, and areas
   for growth.
2. **Manager assessment** — the manager provides a rating and written feedback, informed by their
   own observations and any peer feedback gathered.
3. **Calibration** — ratings are reviewed across a department to help ensure reasonable
   consistency between managers before being finalised.
4. **Review conversation** — the manager and employee discuss the assessment, agree on
   development goals for the next cycle, and document any follow-up actions.

Year-end reviews additionally inform annual compensation and promotion decisions, communicated
separately from the performance conversation itself per standard compensation confidentiality
practice.

## 4. Goal-Setting Framework

CPDA uses a lightweight OKR-style (Objectives and Key Results) framework. Employees set 2-4
objectives per half-year cycle, each with 2-3 measurable key results. Goals should be set
collaboratively with the manager within the first 2 weeks of each cycle and may be adjusted
mid-cycle if business priorities genuinely change, with manager agreement.

## 5. Underperformance and Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

If a manager identifies a sustained performance concern that informal coaching has not resolved,
they may, in consultation with People & Culture, place an employee on a formal Performance
Improvement Plan. A PIP typically:

- Runs for 30, 60, or 90 days depending on the nature of the concern.
- Specifies clear, measurable expectations and the support the employee will receive (e.g.
  additional check-ins, training).
- Includes a mid-point check-in and a final review at the end of the PIP period.
- May result in successful completion (the employee returns to standard performance management),
  an extension in exceptional cases, or, if expectations are not met, further action up to and
  including termination, following the fair-process principles described in the Workplace
  Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09 §5).

A PIP is a performance tool, not a disciplinary one; conduct-related issues are handled instead
under the Workplace Conduct Code's disciplinary process.

## 6. Promotions

Promotions are considered during the year-end review cycle (Section 3) or, for exceptional cases,
on an off-cycle basis with Head of Department and People & Culture approval. A promotion
typically requires demonstrated sustained performance at the next level, not just strong
performance at the current level, and is not automatic based on tenure alone.

## 7. Role Changes and Internal Mobility

Employees interested in moving to a different team or role within CPDA should discuss this with
their current manager first, consistent with the CPDA Way value of being "Open"
(POL-CONDUCT-09 §1). Internal mobility does not require re-doing a full probation period unless
the new role is substantially different in nature or seniority, in which case a shortened
"settling-in" review period (typically 6 weeks) may apply instead.

## 8. Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Standard confirmation.** An employee completes their 3-month probation with solid
performance and no concerns raised. Their manager submits a confirmation review recommending
confirmation; the employee's benefits upgrade to full coverage and Annual Leave begins accruing
retroactively from their original start date, per POL-BEN-06 §1 and POL-PTO-01 §2.

**Example 2 — Probation extension.** An employee shows promise but has not yet met all initial
expectations by the 3-month mark, often due to a slower-than-expected ramp-up on a complex
system. The manager, in consultation with People & Culture, extends probation by 6 weeks with
clear, written expectations for the extension period rather than proceeding straight to
termination.

**Example 3 — PIP leading to successful improvement.** An employee's year-end review flags a
sustained gap in meeting delivery commitments. After informal coaching does not resolve this over
two months, the manager and People & Culture agree a 60-day PIP with specific, measurable
expectations. The employee meets the expectations at the final review and returns to standard
performance management.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I dispute my performance rating?**
A: Yes. Discuss any disagreement with your manager first during the review conversation; if
unresolved, you may request a People & Culture review of how the rating was determined,
consistent with the fair-process principles in POL-CONDUCT-09 §5.

**Q: Does being placed on a PIP affect my eligibility for Annual Leave or other benefits?**
A: No. A PIP is a performance support tool and does not change your employment type, leave
accrual, or benefits eligibility, which continue as normal for a confirmed employee.

**Q: How long after confirmation can I be considered for promotion?**
A: There is no fixed minimum, but promotions (Section 6) require demonstrated performance at the
next level over a meaningful period, which in practice is rarely less than one full review cycle
after confirmation.

**Q: What's the difference between a PIP and the disciplinary process in the Workplace Conduct
Code?**
A: A PIP (Section 5) addresses performance — the quality or pace of work. The Workplace Conduct
Code's disciplinary process (POL-CONDUCT-09 §5) addresses conduct — behavior that violates
workplace standards. The two processes are separate and a PIP does not require a conduct
violation to be initiated.
