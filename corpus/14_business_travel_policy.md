# Business Travel Policy

**Document ID:** POL-TRAVEL-14
**Category:** Finance & Operations
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** Finance Team, in consultation with IT Security
**Applies to:** All CPDA employees travelling on CPDA business

> CPDA is a fictional Singapore government-technology agency created for this course project.
> All policies, figures, and data in this document are synthetic and for educational use only.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs travel undertaken for CPDA business purposes — client meetings, conferences,
inter-agency workshops, and similar activities. It complements, and should be read alongside, the
travel-expense provisions in the Expense & Reimbursement Policy (POL-EXP-04 §4) and the overseas
work provisions in the Remote Work & FWA Policy (POL-FWA-03 §4). Business travel is distinct from
overseas remote work: business travel involves travelling specifically to perform a work task at
the destination (e.g. attending a conference), whereas overseas remote work means continuing
ordinary day-to-day work from a different location.

## 2. Trip Approval

All business travel must be approved by the employee's manager before any booking is made.
International travel additionally requires the traveller to complete a pre-travel checklist
covering destination risk, required vaccinations or health advisories (if applicable), and IT
Security sign-off per Section 5. Domestic travel (within Singapore) generally does not require
the pre-travel checklist, only manager approval.

## 3. Booking

- Flights and accommodation should be booked through CPDA's designated travel booking platform
  where available, which provides built-in approval routing and consolidated billing.
- Economy class is standard for flights under 6 hours; premium economy may be approved by a Head
  of Department for longer flights, consistent with POL-EXP-04 §4.
- Accommodation should be booked at a reasonable standard (3-4 star equivalent) near the meeting
  or conference venue; Finance may query bookings that appear significantly above a reasonable
  market rate for the destination.
- Employees should book at least 2 weeks in advance where possible to secure reasonable fares;
  late bookings for genuinely urgent business needs are permitted but may cost more and should
  still go through the same approval process.

## 4. During the Trip

Employees remain subject to all CPDA policies while travelling for business, including the
Workplace Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09) and the Data Security & Acceptable Use Policy
(POL-SEC-05). Employees should remain reachable during their normal working hours (adjusted for
time zone where reasonable) unless attending sessions that make this impractical, in which case
they should inform their manager in advance.

## 5. Security Considerations

Business travel often involves carrying CPDA equipment and accessing CPDA systems from
unfamiliar networks. Per the Data Security & Acceptable Use Policy (POL-SEC-05 §3), the CPDA VPN
must be used at all times when accessing internal systems while travelling. For destinations on
the IT Security watch list, additional restrictions may apply to production-system access;
travellers should confirm with IT Security during trip approval (Section 2) rather than
discovering restrictions after arrival.

## 6. Combining Business Travel with Personal Time

Employees may extend a business trip with personal Annual Leave (e.g. staying an extra few days
at the destination), subject to manager approval of the Annual Leave portion per the standard
Annual Leave Policy (POL-PTO-01 §3). CPDA covers the business-related portion of travel costs
only; any cost difference caused by extending the trip (e.g. a later return flight) is the
employee's responsibility and should be itemised separately in the expense claim.

## 7. Travel Insurance

CPDA provides travel insurance coverage for all CPDA-approved business trips, arranged
automatically through the designated travel booking platform (Section 3) or, for trips booked
outside the platform, arranged manually by Finance on request. Employees should confirm coverage
is in place before departure for any trip not booked through the standard platform.

## 8. Visa and Immigration

Employees are responsible for ensuring they hold valid travel documents and any visas required
for their destination. Finance can provide a letter of invitation or business purpose letter to
support a visa application on request, but visa costs and the application process itself are the
employee's responsibility, similar to the tax/immigration responsibility described for overseas
remote work in POL-FWA-03 §4 item 5.

## 9. Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Conference attendance.** An employee requests approval to attend a 3-day overseas
conference relevant to their role. Their manager approves the trip; the employee completes the
pre-travel checklist (Section 2), books economy flights and conference-adjacent accommodation
through the travel platform (Section 3), and claims the SGD 80/day per-diem under POL-EXP-04 §4
for meals and incidentals.

**Example 2 — Trip extended with personal leave.** An employee travelling for a 2-day client
workshop wants to stay 3 extra days for personal travel. They request 3 days of Annual Leave for
the extension (POL-PTO-01 §3); CPDA covers the flights and accommodation for the 2 business days
only, and the employee separately books and pays for the extended personal portion.

**Example 3 — Travel to a watch-listed destination.** An employee is asked to travel to a
destination on the IT Security watch list for a short business trip. During trip approval
(Section 2), IT Security flags that access to certain production systems will be temporarily
restricted during the trip per POL-SEC-05 §3; the employee and manager plan the trip itinerary
around this constraint in advance rather than being surprised by it on arrival.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is a one-day local workshop within Singapore considered "business travel" under this
policy?**
A: Day trips within Singapore are generally handled under the domestic travel provisions of the
Expense & Reimbursement Policy (POL-EXP-04 §4) rather than requiring the full approval process in
this document, which is primarily aimed at trips involving accommodation or international travel.

**Q: Who approves business travel if my manager is unavailable?**
A: The same escalation routing as other approvals applies — see the Manager Approval &
Escalation Workflow (POL-APPROVAL-12 §3) for how approvals route when a manager is unavailable
for more than 3 working days.

**Q: Can I choose my own accommodation instead of using the travel booking platform?**
A: Yes, with manager approval, provided the cost and standard are reasonable per Section 3;
booking outside the platform means travel insurance (Section 7) needs to be arranged manually by
Finance.

**Q: Does business travel count toward the overseas remote-work caps in the Remote Work & FWA
Policy?**
A: No. The quarterly/annual overseas caps in POL-FWA-03 §4 apply to ordinary remote work
performed from outside Singapore, not to approved business travel for a specific work purpose
like a conference or client meeting, which follows this policy instead.
