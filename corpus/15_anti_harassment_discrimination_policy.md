# Anti-Harassment & Non-Discrimination Policy

**Document ID:** POL-HARASS-15
**Category:** Conduct & Compliance
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies to:** All CPDA employees, contractors, interns, and third parties in CPDA workplaces

> CPDA is a fictional Singapore government-technology agency created for this course project.
> All policies, figures, and data in this document are synthetic and for educational use only.

## 1. Purpose

This policy provides a detailed, standalone statement of CPDA's commitment to a workplace free
from harassment and discrimination, expanding on the general principles stated in the Workplace
Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09 §2). Where this document and the Workplace Conduct Code overlap, the
more specific guidance in this document applies to harassment and discrimination matters.

## 2. Definitions

**Harassment** is unwelcome conduct — verbal, physical, or visual — that creates an intimidating,
hostile, or offensive work environment, or that unreasonably interferes with an individual's work
performance. This includes, but is not limited to, unwelcome comments or jokes about a protected
characteristic, unwanted physical contact, display of offensive material, and persistent
unwanted attention after being asked to stop.

**Discrimination** is treating an individual less favourably because of a protected
characteristic — including race, religion, gender, age, disability, marital or parental status,
or sexual orientation — in any employment decision, including hiring, promotion, compensation,
or termination.

**Sexual harassment** is a specific form of harassment involving unwelcome sexual advances,
requests for sexual favours, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature, and is
treated with particular seriousness under this policy.

**Retaliation** is any adverse action taken against an individual because they raised a concern
in good faith, participated in an investigation, or supported someone else who did. Retaliation
is itself a serious violation of this policy, independent of the outcome of the underlying
complaint.

## 3. Scope of Coverage

This policy applies to conduct in the physical workplace, in virtual/remote work settings
(video calls, chat tools, email), at work-related social events, and in business travel contexts
(see Business Travel Policy, POL-TRAVEL-14 §4, which confirms the Workplace Conduct Code remains
in effect while travelling). It covers conduct between employees at any level, as well as conduct
involving contractors, vendors, and other third parties encountered through work.

## 4. Reporting

Employees who experience or witness conduct that may violate this policy should report it
through any of the channels described in the Workplace Conduct Code (POL-CONDUCT-09 §3):
their manager, People & Culture, or the confidential HR case channel. A report may be made
verbally or in writing, and does not need to use specific legal terminology — a plain
description of what happened is sufficient to initiate the process described in the HR Case
Intake & Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11).

## 5. Investigation Process

Reports under this policy are automatically treated as Tier 3 under the HR Case Intake &
Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11 §2-3) and are handled by a trained People & Culture case
officer, not by an automated assistant or by the reporting employee's direct manager alone if the
manager is involved in the complaint. A typical investigation:

1. Acknowledges the report within 3 working days (POL-CONDUCT-09 §4).
2. Gathers information from the reporting individual, the individual(s) complained about, and
   any relevant witnesses, with appropriate confidentiality.
3. Reaches a finding based on the information gathered, applying a standard of reasonable
   likelihood given the available evidence.
4. Communicates the outcome to the reporting individual to the extent appropriate, and takes
   corrective action where a violation is found.

Timelines vary by case complexity; People & Culture will provide an estimated timeline after
initial intake and update it if circumstances change.

## 6. Corrective Action

Where an investigation finds a violation, corrective action is proportionate to the severity and
may range from coaching and mandatory training to formal warnings, role changes, or termination,
following the same fair-process principles described in the Workplace Conduct Code
(POL-CONDUCT-09 §5). Repeated or egregious violations are treated more seriously than a single
lower-severity incident, consistent with general disciplinary practice.

## 7. False Complaints

CPDA takes all good-faith reports seriously, even where an investigation does not substantiate
the complaint — raising a genuine concern in good faith is never itself a problem, even if the
investigation outcome is inconclusive. Knowingly making a false report, as distinct from an
unsubstantiated but good-faith one, is itself a conduct violation.

## 8. Manager Responsibilities

Managers who receive a report, or who witness potential harassment or discrimination directly,
must escalate it to People & Culture per Section 4 — managers are not authorised to informally
"handle" a Tier 3 matter themselves, consistent with the escalation criteria in
POL-HRTRIAGE-11 §3. Failing to escalate a known report is itself treated as a serious matter
under this policy.

## 9. Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Direct report to People & Culture.** An employee experiences repeated unwelcome
comments from a colleague after asking them to stop. They report directly to People & Culture
via the confidential HR case channel. Per Section 5, this is immediately treated as Tier 3; a
case officer acknowledges within 3 working days and begins the investigation process.

**Example 2 — Manager receives a report.** An employee tells their manager about a concerning
interaction with a teammate. Per Section 8, the manager must escalate this to People & Culture
rather than attempting to mediate it directly, even if the manager believes it is a minor
misunderstanding.

**Example 3 — Retaliation concern.** An employee who reported a concern several weeks ago notices
their manager has become notably colder and excluded them from meetings they would normally
attend. This pattern itself may constitute retaliation under Section 2 and should be reported as
a new, separate concern to People & Culture.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does this policy apply to something that happened at an off-site team dinner, outside
normal work hours?**
A: Yes. Per Section 3, work-related social events are within scope, regardless of whether they
occur during normal working hours or at the office.

**Q: What if I'm not sure whether what I experienced counts as harassment?**
A: You do not need to be certain before reporting. People & Culture can help assess the situation
during intake (Section 5); raising something in good faith that turns out not to meet the formal
definition is not itself a problem, per Section 7.

**Q: Can an automated HR assistant investigate or decide the outcome of a harassment report?**
A: No. Per Section 5 and the broader HR Case Intake & Escalation Process (POL-HRTRIAGE-11 §7),
an automated assistant may only acknowledge the report with empathy, cite this policy, and
facilitate escalation to a human People & Culture case officer — it must never attempt to
adjudicate or resolve a Tier 3 matter itself.

**Q: Is a single off-colour joke automatically grounds for termination?**
A: Corrective action is proportionate to severity (Section 6); a single lower-severity incident
typically results in coaching or a warning rather than termination, though a pattern of behavior
or a sufficiently severe single incident may warrant stronger action — this is assessed
case-by-case through the investigation process in Section 5.
